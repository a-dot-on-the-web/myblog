I felt so uncomfortable when one of my colleagues came over to my cubicle and abruptly asked me, "Do you live near such and such place?"  I was kind of shocked to hear that at first, and then I learned that she had noticed me on her way home from work because she happened to take the same route for her commute.

Oh, well.

I know of another particular person who takes the same route as I do, so I’ve been careful not to leave or come in around the same time as him.  She’s pretty new to the office, though, and wasn’t on my radar.

Then something else happened.

Recently, she messaged me asking if she could stop by my place on her way home from the office.  I was working from home that day because I was a bit under the weather and didn’t want to spread my germs.  The company had provided lunch that day, and she wanted to bring me some of the leftovers.

I’m very confident that she offered out of genuine kindness.  Yet I still felt very uncomfortable...

I thanked her and told her I really appreciated it, but politely declined, saying that I already had plenty of food at home.  I felt bad afterward, so I texted her again and asked her to please save some for me in the fridge if no one else was taking it.

This incident made me think about boundaries.  Actually, I’ve been thinking about them a lot lately.  I realized that, contrary to what I had assumed about myself, I’ve developed a pretty strong boundary between my work life and my personal life.

Probably I used to be more like her, even though I've always been on the private side.  How and when did I change?  I don't really know.

There was another incident with the same person, actually.  One time, she came over to my cubicle and grabbed a few chips from a bag I had on my desk.  I was a little shocked because they weren’t meant to be shared, and she didn’t even ask if she could have some.

Yet somehow, I could tell there was no ill intention behind it.  From what I’ve observed of her, I think she was genuinely just being friendly.

I froze for a second, then passed her the bag and said, “Aren’t they so good?  Please, have some.  Enjoy!”  I even told her where I bought them.

I was proud of myself, LOL.

Perhaps I’m not fully aware yet that I’m no longer the same person I used to be.  I’m still carrying around an old version of myself, the one whose boundaries were blurred.

Maybe I’m finally growing up into adulthood.

I still want to be kind.  I still want to assume good intentions when I can.  But I also want to respect and listen to that small voice inside me.

Still, moments like these make me nostalgic for the version of myself who could be a little more carefree.
